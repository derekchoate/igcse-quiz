# Calibration notes

Durable, specific facts about how this reader actually reads, learned from feedback
on real edits — not general theory (that lives in `plain-language-toolkit.md` and the
main `SKILL.md`), but the particular things that turned out to matter for *him*.

Read this before an edit. Add to it after feedback — a correction ("still too dense
even though it passed the word-count target," "that repeat was on purpose, don't cut
it") or a confirmation that something worked ("the four-short-sentences rewrite of the
Module 23 hero landed well"). Keep entries short and concrete; link back to the module
or passage where it came up so a future edit can find the precedent.

This file starts empty on purpose. It's meant to fill in over real edits, the same way
`personal-humanizer`'s `voice-profile.md` fills in over real rewrites — general
guidance up front, specifics learned from doing the actual work.

## Entries

- **Number items when a sentence states an exact count; use bullets otherwise.**
  Explicit feedback, from the "one of three roads" bus example in Module 17: since the
  intro sentence names an exact count, each item should be numbered ("1.", "2.", "3.")
  as it's introduced. Refined immediately after: numbering is only for a stated count —
  a list with no promised total should use a bullet instead, so the number doesn't
  imply a total that was never claimed. Baked into `SKILL.md` step 4 directly.

- **Don't highlight a sustained working metaphor, even though it's a "term."** Found
  while reviewing Module 24: "compartment" is the module's plain-English stand-in for
  an array element and appears dozens of times — bolding every occurrence would bold a
  large fraction of the page. The highlight-every-time rule is for named technical
  terms (array, address bus, SUBSTRING), not a metaphor word reused as ordinary
  vocabulary throughout a whole module. Baked into `SKILL.md` step 4 as a carve-out on
  the highlighting rule, immediately after it.

- **Highlight terms every time they appear, not just at first mention.** Explicit
  direction, prompted by noticing that Module 17's nudges reuse "Address bus," "Data
  bus," and "Control bus" repeatedly without `<strong>` after the first mention in the
  main paragraph (see the `n2b` nudge, for example — plain text on every repeat). Keep
  the highlight on every appearance across the whole module, including nudges and the
  reflection card, not only where the term is first defined. Baked into `SKILL.md`
  step 4 directly.

- **Active voice is not just "by default" — treat it as a hard rule.** Explicit
  direction: all language should be active, not passive, with no "the actor doesn't
  matter here" exception the way `personal-humanizer` allows. Baked into
  `plain-language-toolkit.md` and `SKILL.md` step 3 directly rather than left as a
  calibration-only note, since it applies to every edit, not just a specific passage.
  The one carve-out is a Cambridge syllabus definition that's passively worded in the
  official appendix — keep that exact, and put the active voice around it instead.
