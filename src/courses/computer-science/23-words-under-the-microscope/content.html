Every word a program ever handles — a name, a password, a search box — is just a row of characters sitting in numbered positions. UCASE("quiet") reads back "QUIET" — every letter flips together, never just the first. LCASE("QUIET") reads back "quiet" the same way, in reverse. And when a program compares "Cat" to "cat" directly, it gets FALSE — a capital C and a lowercase c are simply different characters to a machine. UCASE both sides first ("CAT" vs "CAT") and the comparison becomes TRUE. That's exactly why comparisons so often UCASE everything before comparing.

RANDOM() takes no information in at all — it just hands back a decimal somewhere between 0 and 1, every single time.
