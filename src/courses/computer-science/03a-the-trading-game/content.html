Grouping is what makes quantity readable — and you've been using it since you first counted on your fingers. Every written number is just a tidy note about groups. That's the whole idea this room is built on.

There's no catch — press the button, and afterwards notice where your eyes go: they stop scanning beans and start counting rows. That switch in your head is the discovery.

This is a trading machine. It has exactly one rule: a cup is never allowed to hold ten. The moment a tenth bean lands, all ten bundle into one bag and slide one cup to the left. That's it — the entire rule. Every number you've ever written down is this machine's paperwork.

And now the question that matters: the tens cup is holding 9 bags. How many beans is that, really?

9 bags × 10 beans = 90 beans. That's the whole reason a 9 sitting in the second cup means ninety — the digit counts bags, and the cup sets what each bag holds.

Everything on this bench opens with a tap. Bags contain either beans or smaller bags — there's nothing else they could contain.

Trading at sixteen, a single cup can legally hold up to fifteen — more than the digits 0–9 can write. So counts of ten to fifteen borrow the letters A–F. You've met these: they're the sixteen symbols from Module 2, and this is the machine they come from.

Worth knowing: in Switch Arithmetic, when a column's total spills into the next column — the carry — that is this machine trading two-for-one. Same move, new costume.

Notice what you just rebuilt

The grown-up name for today is place value — the idea underneath every number system humans have ever used. Most people run it their whole lives without once seeing the gears. You just took it apart on a bench: a digit is a count of bags, a cup's value is what one of its bags holds, and the trade rule decides everything else. Decimal, binary, hexadecimal — one game, three costumes. Nobody decided the third bulb is worth 4. Two twos did.

If you wander back to Switch Arithmetic, the carries there will look different now — they're trades, and you've made dozens. No rush at all — the stars keep.
