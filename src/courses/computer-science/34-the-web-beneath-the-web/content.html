The internet and the web are not the same thing. One is roads. One is what gets delivered on them.

And every address you type sets off a scavenger hunt you never see — a name gets turned into a number, a request goes out, and a page comes back together in front of you, all before you've finished blinking. Tonight you take that whole hidden trip apart, one stop at a time: what's actually the internet versus what's actually the web, what's hiding inside a URL, who translates a name into a number, what a padlock icon really promises, and what a website remembers about you after you leave.

Here's the distinction this whole module leans on, stated plainly before anything else depends on it. The internet is the infrastructure — the cables, satellites and routers physically carrying data from one device to another, the same way roads carry vehicles. The World Wide Web is the collection of websites and pages you reach through it — one particular way of using those roads, the same way a food delivery is one particular thing a road can carry. The internet was carrying data — email, files, all sorts — years before the web existed at all. Everything below is either a fact about the roads or a fact about one specific kind of delivery that travels on them.

HTTP (hypertext transfer protocol) is the set of rules your browser and a web server use to ask for and send back pages. HTTPS (hypertext transfer protocol secure) is the exact same job, with one addition: everything exchanged gets locked, using the same public-padlock trick Module 15 built — a public key that only ever locks, and a private key, kept by the server alone, that's the only thing that ever unlocks it. A cookie is a small piece of text a website asks your browser to remember on its behalf — it isn't a program, it can't run anything and it can't infect anything on its own; it's just a note. Think of it as a wristband handed out the moment you arrive. A session cookie is a wristband stamped "today only" — it evaporates the instant you close the tab. A persistent cookie is stamped with a real expiry date, sometimes weeks or months away, and survives long after the tab is gone. Press the button below and watch the difference for yourself.
