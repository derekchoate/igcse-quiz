Pictures & Sound from Numbers
✦ 0 stars only ever go up

Every photo you've ever taken is a spreadsheet of numbers wearing a costume.

You already know a byte can spell a letter. It can just as easily spell a tiny square of colour — millions of them, side by side, and suddenly it's a photograph. In the next twenty minutes you'll paint with bulbs, watch a picture blur into blocks as its resolution drops, and discover that sound gets turned into numbers by simply measuring a wiggle very, very often. Same trick as always. Different costume.

Same deal as always: no marks, no timer, no wrong answers, nothing to hand in. Flip switches, follow curiosity, take a nudge whenever you like. Stars can never be taken away.

A picture is just a grid of tiny squares — pixels — and the simplest version of each pixel is a single bulb: lit or dark, exactly like Module 1. Paint the grid below any way you like. Watch the panel beside it mirror your picture in raw 1s and 0s — that panel isn't decoration, it's genuinely what's stored on disk.

what's actually stored

While you paint, try to…

Try lighting a whole row — watch that row's line in the numbers panel turn to solid 1s.
Clear the grid back to all-dark, then build a small pattern of your own. Every square you light is one more 1 stored somewhere.

One bulb per pixel only gets you black or white. Give a pixel more bulbs and it can hold more shades in between — 2 bulbs make 4 shades, 3 bulbs make 8, and so on. Slide the depth control below and watch a single pixel go from a blunt on/off switch to something closer to a real photograph.

Sound is a wiggle in the air, and a computer can only ever measure it at a moment in time — never the whole smooth curve at once. The grey line below is that smooth wiggle. Tap points along it to take samples, and watch the amber line — the version a computer would actually store — connect the dots you chose.

Try both extremes…

Tap only two or three points, spread far apart — watch how much of the wiggle gets flattened out between them.
Now tap nearly every point available. The amber line starts hugging the grey one almost exactly — more samples, truer sound.

You've now watched more pixels and more bits both cost you file size. Time to say the whole rule in one sentence, in your own words. Pick a word for each gap below — there's more than one honest way to finish it.

More pixels or more bits per pixel means ___ picture quality, and ___ files.

Both blanks move the same direction: more information always costs more room to store it.
There's no version of this sentence where quality goes up and size doesn't. That's the whole trade-off, in one line.

Notice what just happened — a fifth time

Twenty minutes ago, "a photo is just numbers" might have sounded like a slogan. Now you've painted in raw bits, watched a single pixel earn its shades, dropped a picture's resolution and watched the file size follow it down, turned a smooth sound wave into a row of samples, and written the whole trade-off yourself, in your own words.

Whenever you're curious, the next discovery is working out exactly how big a whole song or photo really is — down to the byte. No rush at all — the stars keep.
