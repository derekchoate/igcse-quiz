Before a single line of code: how do you get a wish into steps a machine can follow?

Every piece of software starts the same way: someone wants something, and it's fuzzy. "I want an app that splits a bill fairly." Nobody can code that sentence directly — it has to be broken down first. Tonight you'll watch one small wish travel through the four stages every program passes through, then get properly hands-on: categorising the steps a real gadget takes by what it takes in, what it works out, what it hands back, and what it remembers.

A plant-watering gadget's routine is small enough to hold in your head all at once. Everything it does falls into exactly one of four jobs: something arrives from outside, something gets worked out, something goes back out, or something gets kept for later. Categorise each step below by its job.

Ask: does this piece come from outside the system, get worked on, go out to a person, or get kept for later?
A sensor reading arrives from outside — that's Input. Work done on that reading is Process. What it hands back is Output. What it holds onto is Storage.
