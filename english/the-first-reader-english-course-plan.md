# The First Reader — English Course Plan
### Cambridge IGCSE First Language English 0500 (targeting the 2027–2029 syllabus) as a discovery series

Third course in the project. Inherits the full design contract, house style, Foundations Layer, Phase 2 Bridge architecture, and `CLAUDE.md`. Companion documents: `writing-feedback-tool-spec.md` (the reading companion — required by Strand F rung 4). **Verify every assessment detail against the official 0500 syllabus for 2027–2029 before building** — Paper 1 restructures in that cycle (four 20-mark questions, ~1,400 words of text, a comprehension question folded into the summary task), and this plan targets that structure.

Route: private candidate → Paper 1 (Reading) + Paper 2 (Directed Writing and Composition). No coursework; the optional Speaking & Listening endorsement is not taken unless he later wants it (it does not affect the grade).

---

## Part A — The English contract (additions to the standing design contract)

English is a **judgement domain**: half the exam is writing assessed on quality bands, and there is no state to match. These rules handle that honestly.

### A1 · No verdicts, anywhere.
Nothing in this course scores, grades, bands, or ranks his writing — including the reading companion (see its spec: never a score, never a rewrite, one growth experiment at a time). Quality is learned through **comparison and choice**: two versions of a sentence side by side, which hits harder and why; his draft beside a model *unfolding next to it, never replacing it*. Mark-band language exists only in the Bridge, behind its consent doors.

### A2 · The dialect layer (course-wide engine feature).
Exam questions are written in an idiom — "why do you think the writer…?" cannot literally be answered and is code for "what does this make a reader think, feel, or picture?" The course treats this as **a dialect he decodes**, never as a defect in his reading:
- **The Translate button** — the Rebuild button's sibling — appears wherever a conventional question appears, in any module, forever: one tap reveals the literal translation beneath. Early modules show both by default; the idiomatic form gradually becomes the surface with translation always one tap away. Scaffold fades; it is never removed.
- **Answer shapes** attach to every question type: explicit skeletons ("The word ___ suggests ___ because it makes the reader imagine ___") — stated conventions per the standing rule, and also simply what earns credit.
- **The honesty exhibit** (Q1): the course says plainly that these questions are imprecise idioms, and decodes them anyway. For a precise thinker, acknowledging the imprecision is the difference between trusting the course and privately concluding it's sloppy.
- The reading companion phrases its own questions literally and models the translation whenever it uses craft idiom (already in its spec).

### A3 · Doors, re-costumed.
The three-doors principle becomes: **HEAR it** (passages available read aloud — browser speech synthesis, optional always), **SEE it** (the text as a marked-up object: highlights, colour, annotation), **BUILD it** (meaning or writing assembled from draggable parts). Doors still open in sequence from a stable base.

### A4 · His writing is his.
Content sovereignty: subjects, opinions, darkness, strangeness — all his. Creative work is received as literature (the companion's spec already binds this). No topic policing beyond genuine safety, no tidying of his voice into blandness, and personal material is never required — every prompt has an impersonal alternative.

### A5 · The reading diet is human territory.
Volume reading is the highest-impact input to this entire course and lives **outside the software**: an interest-led list curated by Derek, maintained offline, private to the two of them. Audiobook-paired reading counts fully. Modules use short excerpts; the diet supplies the miles. The course never assigns reading, tracks pages, or gamifies books — reading must belong to pleasure, not to the star system.

### A6 · Banned, in the spirit of A12 (maths).
Red-pen culture in any visual form; itemised error lists; "reading age" or level talk; PEE/PEEL drilled as empty ritual (answer shapes are offered as *scaffolds with visible purpose*, and rung progression retires them); forced reading aloud (HEAR is always the machine's job unless he chooses otherwise).

### A7 · Figures are formulas.
English has formulas; school just stopped teaching them as such. Classical rhetoric catalogued them for two millennia: named sentence-patterns with gaps that reliably produce effects — the short sentence after long ones, the rule of three, anaphora, "not X, but Y", the delayed reveal. **Structure causes effect, independent of content**, which is what makes them formula-like — so they get the full Museum treatment per `blueprint-architecture.md`:
- **The derivation exhibit:** take a published sentence that demonstrably lands (a *specimen*); hollow it into gaps; pour HIS content in; the effect survives. Scrubbing between specimen and his version — different words, same punch — is the proof that the pattern, not the words, carries the effect. The Rebuild button links back to the specimen.
- **Every figure is one library entry read in two directions:** Paper 1 direction (spot it in a text, name its effect — writer's-effects marks) and Paper 2 direction (deploy it in his own writing — composition marks). One understanding, credited twice. This reframes Paper 1 analysis as *reading the blueprints other writers used*.
- **Stress tests include when-NOT-to:** deploying a figure where it clangs (the rule of three opening a formal report) is part of owning it.
- The canonical figure set (~15–20 entries) lives in the architecture document's English catalogue — tutor/build-facing, never issued as a list; each figure is minted at its first genuine encounter, mostly in F2, R4 and R5.

### A8 · Paper 1 carries writing marks.
Under the 2027 structure, the Reading paper awards real writing marks (5 in the summary, 10 in the extended response). Consequence: E-strand accuracy and Forge craft are Paper 1 assets, and the Bridge's harvest framing must credit them there — the Forge is never "the Paper 2 strand".

## Part C — Module plans

Format — **Serves | Needs** · Hook · Doors · Signature · Watch for. Excerpts throughout are short (fair-dealing length), varied per the generalisation rule, and chosen warm — wildlife writing, travel, games journalism, and fiction in the spirit of his own shelf are all fair game.

---

**Q1 · The Dialect of Questions** — P1 & P2 | nothing; the course's front door after the welcome
Hook: "Exam questions are written in a dialect. 'Why do you think the writer used this word?' cannot literally be answered — nobody reads minds, and 'because she chose to' is perfectly true. The question is code. Codes can be cracked, catalogued, and owned." Doors: SEE question-and-translation pairs; BUILD the codebook himself (match idiomatic questions to plain meanings — state-matching); HEAR them read. Signature: **the field linguist's codebook** — the finite set of 0500 question conventions ("why do you think…", "what impressions…", "how does the writer suggest…", "explain what the writer means by…", "comment on how…", plus the short-answer Reading conventions the audit confirmed belong in this codebook: **own-words meaning** of a word/phrase, its reverse **"find the word/phrase that means…"** synonym-match — a named 2027 type — the **"list/give/identify N…"** multi-point retrieval convention, and the causal **"explain why…"** convention) each decoded to its literal meaning, then the reverse-engineering game: shown answers that earned credit, deduce what the question was really asking. Ends with the **honesty exhibit**: the course states outright that the idiom is imprecise and that his literal reading was correct — the dialect, not his mind, is the crooked thing being straightened. The Translate button is introduced here and never leaves. Watch for: treating translations as more memorisation — the codebook is *his* artefact, assembled not issued; new question phrasings met later route back here via secret passage.

**Q2 · The Code of Show-Don't-Tell** — reading & writing | Q1
Hook: "Writers almost never say 'she was angry'. They say she slammed the door. Feelings travel through actions, weather, and objects — a code with learnable rules, catalogued here." Doors: SEE paired lines (told vs shown) with the feeling highlighted in one and *carried* in the other; BUILD the code chart (action → feeling pairings, drag-matched); HEAR delivery differences. Signature: **the carrier chart** — a growing, explicit catalogue of how emotion is transmitted (actions, body, weather, objects, dialogue subtext), built from short examples; then the decoder game (given a shown line, read the feeling — state-matching against a warm option set that allows defensible alternatives, "also works"). Watch for: over-application (not every raindrop is grief — include neutral examples); the chart hardening into one-to-one lookup (multiple feelings per carrier, always).

**R1 · Reading the Room** — P1 retrieval | Q1
Hook: "The first skill is honest looking: what does the text actually say? Detectives call it evidence; you'll collect it by hand." Doors: SEE highlighting as the core mechanic (finding the answer *is* selecting the sentence — state-matching in its purest reading form); HEAR the passage; BUILD answers from retrieved fragments. Signature: **the evidence highlighter** — questions answered by painting the supporting words; distractor questions whose answer is *not present* teach the difference between stated and assumed (a literal thinker's home turf, and he'll be good at it — say so). Watch for: paraphrase blindness (the text says "furious", the question says "angry" — synonym bridges taught explicitly, conventions-stated rule); over-highlighting (the "smallest sufficient evidence" game); multi-point questions ("list/give/identify N…") are the same retrieval skill — one distinct location per point, no detail reused (the audit's E3 home); the reverse synonym direction ("find the word/phrase that means…") runs the synonym bridge backwards and is drilled explicitly here (the audit's E2 reverse / 2027 cloze convention).

**R2 · The Inference Engine** — P1 | R1, Q2
Hook: "Now the second kind of reading: what the text means but never says. Not guessing — deduction, using the code from Q2." Doors: SEE inference chains built as visible steps (clue + code rule → conclusion — the footprints panel, re-costumed for reading); BUILD chains from parts; HEAR tone. Signature: **the deduction board** — each inference constructed as an explicit chain he assembles: quoted clue → carrier-chart rule → inferred meaning; nothing is ever "just obvious", every inference has a visible mechanism. Degrees of certainty introduced (what the text *proves* vs *suggests* — honest epistemology, his strength). Watch for: inference as licensed guessing (the chain requirement prevents it); single-clue overreach (strong inferences cite two clues — build it into the board); causal "explain why…" questions are R2 work aimed at *why* rather than *what it means* — the same deduction board handles them unchanged (the audit's E4 home).

**R3 · Words Carry Charge** — P1, feeds F5 | R1
Hook: "'Slithered' and 'walked' can describe the same journey. One of them makes your skin crawl. Every word carries a charge — and writers pick on purpose." Doors: SEE the swap machine (one sentence, one slot, a wheel of near-synonyms — the sentence's feeling shifts live as he spins); BUILD charge-sorted word sets (warm↔cold, fast↔slow, safe↔menacing sliders); HEAR the versions aloud. Signature: **the connotation lab** — the swap machine plus charge sliders; the core Paper 1 answer shape born here ("___ suggests ___ because it makes the reader imagine ___", with the Translate button's sibling: a shape-reminder tap). Watch for: denotation/connotation blur; "suggests" answers that just re-state the word (the *because it makes the reader imagine* clause is mandatory in the shape for exactly this reason).

**R4 · The Writer's Toolbox I** — P1 | R3
Hook: "Some effects come from single words; others from small machines built of words — comparisons that smuggle one thing inside another." Doors: SEE simile/metaphor/personification as literal picture-machines (the compared things rendered visually merging); BUILD comparisons from part-trays; HEAR them. Signature: **the image workshop** — identify the machine (drag-pair, warm "also works" tolerance where lines blur — metaphor vs personification honestly overlaps and the course says so), then the effect question via R3's shape: not *naming* the technique but *what it makes the reader picture/feel* (naming alone earns little; the workshop's whole grain runs toward effect). **Mint-moments throughout** (per A7): each comparison-machine genuinely understood mints a two-way figure entry — spot it reading, deploy it writing — via the F2 bench mechanic, hollowing a specimen he just analysed. Watch for: technique-spotting as the end goal (the exam's most common wasted answer); "it makes it more interesting" as a void effect — banned phrase, gently, with the shape as the alternative.

**R5 · The Writer's Toolbox II** — P1 | R4, F2 pairs well
Hook: "Meaning also travels through sound and shape: sentences that punch, sentences that wander, sounds that hiss. You'll hear it before you name it." Doors: HEAR first, deliberately (this module's door order inverts — sound is the point); SEE rhythm visualised (sentence lengths as bars, repetition as echo highlights); BUILD contrasting versions. Signature: **the rhythm bench** — passages with sentence-length graphs beneath; the short-sentence-after-long-ones effect *felt* then named; alliteration/sibilance/repetition as audible textures with the effect shape applied. **Mint-moments throughout** (per A7): rhythm figures met here (the short landing, anaphora, the accelerating list) mint as two-way entries at the F2 bench. Watch for: counting devices instead of reading them; alliteration "makes it flow" void answers (same ban, same alternative).

**R6 · Impressions & Evidence** — P1 | R2, R3
Hook: "'What impressions do you get of the old man?' — dialect for: what do you come to think of him, and which words did it to you. Claim plus evidence plus mechanism. You own all three parts." Doors: SEE impression-building as accumulation (each highlighted detail nudges a live 'portrait' summary); BUILD full answers from claim/quote/because tiles (rung 1 of analysis writing); HEAR the passage. Signature: **the portrait builder** — impressions assembled as claim + smallest-sufficient quote + mechanism, the three parts colour-coded everywhere (this colour-coding IS the X2 annotation ritual, seeded years early). Includes the **"whose feelings are these?" discovery** (audit amendment E1): the crucial split between a *character's* feelings (carried by the Q2 code) and the *writer's own attitude* toward the subject (tone, stance, word-charge tilt — the 2027 papers ask "assess the writer's attitude" as a named question type). Mechanic: passages where character and writer disagree — a narrator describing a "hero" in words that quietly sneer — sorted on a two-sided board; mints "the attitude question" into the codebook, with D3's persuasion-lab instincts (his fact-checking streak) as congenial ground. Watch for: impressions without text anchors; quotes doing the claiming (his words, then the text's words — the tile grammar enforces the order); attributing a character's feelings to the writer (the two-sided board exists for this).

**R7 · The Art of Leaving Out** — P1 summary | R1
Hook: "Summary is the professional's skill of ruthless relevance: keep exactly what the question asks about, lose everything else, and say it in your own words. It's a filtering machine, and you're the filter." Doors: SEE relevance-highlighting against a stated focus (the question defines the filter — literal, explicit); BUILD the own-words machine (each kept point re-said via synonym tiles, then free rephrase at higher rungs); HEAR nothing needed — two doors, honestly. Signature: **the distillery** — passage in, focus stated, he keeps/discards points (state-matching against the mark-scheme's point list, with "also creditable" tolerance), then re-voices the kept points; a live word-count that *never alarms*, just informs. The new-cycle comprehension sub-question folds in here per the 2027 structure. Watch for: lifting whole phrases (the own-words stage is structural, not optional); including vivid-but-irrelevant details (the filter question re-shown on every keep/discard).

**R8 · Effects, Written Up** — P1 extended response | R3–R6, F1–F3
Hook: "Everything from the toolbox modules, composed into the written-up form Paper 1 wants — using the Forge's rungs, because analysis is writing too." Doors: BUILD (rung 1: assemble model analyses from tiles including true-but-creditless distractors, per the Bridge's Six-Mark Workshop pattern); SEE skeletons (rung 3: claim/quote/because paragraphs with the colour-coding); free writing at rung 4 with the reading companion reading his *analysis* (its spec covers this — reader response to his reading of the reading). Signature: the rung ladder applied to analytical prose, with the **choose-your-evidence** twist: passages rich enough that different selections are all defensible, so his answer is *his*, never the answer. Watch for: technique-listing relapse; quotes over three words unexamined; the companion drifting into scoring register (its spec forbids it — bug-report if seen).

**R9 · Reading Long** — P1 stamina & structure | R1–R2, reading diet underway
Hook: "Exam texts run to pages, and whole texts have architecture: openings that plant, middles that turn, endings that land. Structure is a map you can learn to see." Doors: SEE paragraph-role mapping (each paragraph tagged by job: establish, complicate, shift, land — drag-tagged); BUILD scrambled texts back into order (structure felt through reassembly); HEAR long passages while following highlighted text (the stamina-builder — paired audio-visual reading, normalised here because it's normalised in his diet). Signature: **the architecture map** — longer excerpts (600–900 words, building toward the 2027 cycle's ~1,400-word diet) with structural annotation; "why does the writer put this HERE?" as a dialect question with its translation (what does the placement do to the reader's journey). Watch for: stamina treated as character rather than training (it's laps, and the module says so); structure questions answered with content summaries.

**R10 · Borrowed Voices (the persona response)** — P1 Q4, 20 marks: 10 reading + 10 writing | R1, R7, F9 (register dial)
Hook: "The Reading paper's final question hands you a role: 'You are Annie. Write your responses to the interviewer's three questions.' It looks like it wants imagination. It actually wants something more precise: **acting from a script the text provides**." That framing carries the module — no "imagine you are her", which asks a literal thinker to conjure; instead an explicit three-station pipeline. Doors: SEE model responses beside their source texts with every borrowed detail highlighted back to its origin; BUILD the pipeline (below); HEAR persona voice vs the text's narrator voice. Signature: **the script harvest** — station 1: each bullet prompt becomes a filter (R7's distillery move) and he harvests the text details that answer it; station 2: the register dial (F9) set to the persona — who they are, who's asking, how they'd speak; station 3: Forge rungs on the response itself, details woven in as the persona's own knowledge, companion reading rung-4 attempts. Blueprint minted: *bullets → harvest per bullet → set the voice → respond, grounding every claim in the text*. The reading marks come from the harvest's coverage and inference; the writing marks from voice and accuracy — show him the two-channel credit openly at Bridge stage. Watch for: invented content the text can't support (the highlight-back-to-origin mechanic makes grounding visible); the persona knowing things they couldn't (dramatic-irony trap); narrator voice bleeding into persona voice (the two-voice HEAR door trains the ear); bullets unevenly covered (the three filters each demand their harvest).

**R11 · The Full Case** — P1 rehearsal, re-skinned | all R
Hook: "One complete investigation: fresh texts, every skill, home clothes." Signature: a full Paper 1-shaped set (per the 2027 structure: the four-question shape, Borrowed Voices finale included) rendered entirely in module mechanics — highlighting, deduction boards, the distillery, portrait tiles, the script harvest, one written-up response — untimed, nudged, star-carrying. This is Bridge stage P1 for English, built into the course proper; its debrief (with Derek) is the natural moment to discuss opening the Bridge's later doors. Watch for: treating it as The Test in disguise (the framing, pacing, and mechanics must be indistinguishable from any other module — that's the point).

**F1 · The Forge Opens** — P2 | Q2 helpful
Hook: "Writing is built, not summoned. The Forge works in four rungs — assemble, complete, skeleton, free — and you choose the rung, always." Doors: BUILD is the strand's native door — rung 1 here: assemble strong paragraphs from sentence tiles (including flat variants of good sentences — feeling the difference IS the lesson); SEE two-version comparisons (A1's mechanic debuts: which lands harder, and what's the visible difference); HEAR both versions. Signature: **the comparison bench + the rung dial** — the Forge's permanent furniture. The rung dial is his control, stated plainly: dropping a rung is craft, not retreat (professionals outline; outlining is rung 3). Watch for: rung 4 reached too fast out of pride (the dial's framing exists for this); tile answers treated as the only good order (multiple orders often work — "also works" applies).

**F2 · The Figures Bench** — P1 & P2 | F1
Hook: "Writers have formulas. Nobody calls them that anymore, but rhetoric spent two thousand years cataloguing sentence-patterns that reliably work on readers — and a pattern with gaps is exactly the kind of thing you collect." This is the minting workshop for figures (per A7 and the architecture): the bench where **specimen → hollow → swap → the effect survives** happens for the first time and then repeatedly. Doors: SEE the specimen exhibit — a published sentence that lands, scrub-controlled as it hollows into gaps and refills with HIS content, the punch surviving the transplant (the proof that structure, not words, carries the effect); BUILD sentences from movable part-trays (front-load the storm, or land on it — clause order as a physical act); HEAR every version, because rhythm is the point. Signature: **the bench itself** — one idea rebuilt five ways; the first mints (the deliberate short sentence, the rule of three, the contrast pair) each entering his library as **two-way entries**: spot-it-reading (Paper 1 credit) and deploy-it-writing (Paper 2 credit) on the same card. Stress tests include when-NOT-to: the rule of three opening a formal report, felt clanging. Sentence-length bars (R5's visualisation) run under his own drafts. Watch for: complexity worship (longer ≠ better — the bench celebrates the four-word landing); figures deployed as decoration rather than for effect (the two-way card's effect line is mandatory); comma-spliced "long sentences" (E2's passage opens from here when spotted).

**F3 · Paragraph Machines** — P2 | F1
Hook: "Paragraphs are rooms: each has one job, a doorway in, and a doorway out." Doors: SEE paragraph jobs tagged (R9's roles, production side); BUILD paragraphs around given topic sentences, then link rooms with doorway phrases (a catalogued, explicit set — conventions stated); HEAR flow. Signature: **the room builder** — draft-space that renders his paragraphs as literal rooms on a corridor, doorways (links) visible or missing; one-idea-per-room checked by *him* naming each room's job in three words. Watch for: rooms with two jobs; corridor with no doors (links); topic sentences buried mid-room.

**F4 · Descriptive I: The Camera** — P2 composition | F2; companion live from here
Hook: "Description is controlled attention: you hold the reader's eye like a camera — wide, then close, then closer. Where you point it is the writing." Doors: SEE camera-move planning on an image (literal: an explorable scene he plots shots across — wide establish, pan, zoom to a detail); BUILD shot-lists into skeleton paragraphs (rung 3 native here); free drafts to the companion at rung 4. Signature: **the shot planner** — description scaffolded as cinematography, five senses as lenses (sound and smell as underused lenses, said plainly); the static-scene trap pre-empted by the "one small movement" rule (something in the scene moves; time passes slightly). Watch for: narrative invasion (description that becomes plot — the brief's translation makes the difference explicit); adjective pile-ups (the R3 charge lab's lesson: one charged word beats four flat ones).

**F5 · Descriptive II: Atmosphere** — P2 composition | F4, R3
Hook: "Same street, two moods: welcome home, or something's wrong. Atmosphere is the charge lab, writing side." Doors: SEE paired-mood model passages diverging from a shared scene; BUILD mood-swapped versions (given a safe-scene sentence, re-charge it — the swap machine in reverse); companion drafts at rung 4. Signature: **the atmosphere dial** — one scene drafted twice under opposite briefs, Q2's carriers doing the transmitting (weather, objects, small actions); his two versions side-by-side is A1's comparison mechanic applied to his own work — the safest possible self-comparison, because both are his and the *difference* is the achievement. Watch for: mood stated instead of carried ("it was creepy" — Q2's passage opens); uniform charge (atmosphere needs contrast to register — one warm detail makes the cold colder).

**F6 · Narrative I: Shapes of Stories** — P2 composition | F3
Hook: "Exam stories fail from shapelessness, not from imagination. There are a handful of shapes that fit 400 words — schemas, like the maths stories, but for telling instead of solving." Doors: SEE story shapes as visual arcs (the moment-of-change, the circle-back, the two-timelines-meet — small catalogue, explicitly finite); BUILD given stories' events onto shape templates; skeleton drafts per shape. Signature: **the shape shelf** — narrative schemas as planning objects; openings and endings as bound pairs chosen *before* drafting (the ending-first trick, taught as insider knowledge); scale honesty — 400-word stories are about *one moment*, and the shelf's shapes all enforce smallness. Watch for: epic scope (the one-moment rule); shapes as cages (the shelf is a launch, and shape-breaking on purpose is celebrated at rung 4).

**F7 · Narrative II: Voice** — P2 composition | F6, Q2
Hook: "The last craft layer: whose eyes, whose voice, and showing instead of telling — the Q2 code, transmitting side." Doors: SEE the same scene in first vs third person, past vs present (comparison bench); BUILD told→shown conversions (given "she was terrified", produce the carriers — his chart, used in reverse); companion at rung 4 for full stories. Signature: **the transmitter** — Q2's carrier chart as a writing tool: feelings assigned to actions/objects/weather from his own catalogue; dialogue as character-revelation with E3's mechanics adjacent. Watch for: head-hopping (one viewpoint per story at this length — stated convention); shown *and* told redundancy ("she slammed the door angrily" — the adverb tax, named with wit).

**F8 · Directed I: The Forms** — P2 directed | F3
Hook: "Directed writing hands you the content and tests the container: letter, speech, article, report — each a form with knowable conventions. Knowable is your favourite kind." Doors: SEE annotated exemplars of each form, conventions explicitly flagged (greeting, sign-off, address-the-room, headline — a checklist per form, given not inferred); BUILD form-feature sorting (which features belong to which container — state-matching); skeleton drafts per form. Signature: **the container cabinet** — the finite form set with explicit convention cards; the same content poured into two containers to feel what the form changes. Watch for: form features as decoration on unchanged prose (the pour-across comparison exposes it); invented conventions (the cards are the authority — no guessing required, ever).

**F9 · Directed II: The Register Dial** — P2 directed | F8
Hook: "Formality is a dial, not a switch — and the question always tells you where to set it: who's reading, and why." Doors: SEE the same request at four dial settings (mate-text → council letter); BUILD register-swaps (given formal, produce warm; given casual, produce official — the swap machine for whole sentences); HEAR the settings (register is loudest aloud). Signature: **the register dial** — audience and purpose extracted from the brief as two explicit slots (the dialect layer applied to P2 briefs: the Translate button works here too), then the dial set and the draft checked against it by comparison, not verdict. Watch for: register drift mid-piece (the dial shown beside the draft-space, constant); formality as stiffness (formal ≠ frozen — the exemplars prove warmth survives the dial).

**F10 · Directed III: Answering the Source** — P2 directed | F9, R7
Hook: "The full directed task: read source material, take a position, and write the container — using the reading strand's tools on the way in and the Forge's on the way out. Both halves of the course, one task." Doors: SEE source-mining as R1/R7 mechanics (highlight the usable points against the brief's focus); BUILD position-plus-points plans (his stance, their evidence, the container chosen); companion at rung 4 on complete pieces. Signature: **the assembly line** — source → mined points → stance → container → register → draft, each station a tool he already owns, the pipeline visible (W3's story-pipeline, English edition — and the same generalisation engine).
